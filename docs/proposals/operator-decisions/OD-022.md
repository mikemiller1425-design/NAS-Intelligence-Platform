## The decision being requested

What operator authentication model does V1 use for approvals?

## Why it matters

Resolving the approval-binding audit finding required binding every approval to an authenticated operator identity — but V1 scope never stated an authentication requirement. The Sentinel, the lowest-privilege component, was the only component required to authenticate; the operator holding destructive authority was not. **This is a scope addition, and it is the operator's to accept or reshape.**

## Safe options

**1. Local single-user, loopback-only, approval-time re-authentication**

A principal registry on control storage, loopback-bound surfaces, a fresh factor at the moment of approval, journalled auth events, session binding.

*Tradeoff:* Proposed in the approval-binding model. Modest to implement; sufficient for a single-operator deployment; no external dependency.

**2. OS keychain or biometric unlock at approval time**

Delegate the factor to the platform.

*Tradeoff:* Better ergonomics, but ties the design to one operating system and complicates the audit trail.

**3. No authentication in V1; rely on physical machine access**

Simplest.

*Tradeoff:* **Not recommended.** It leaves the destructive gate unauthenticated while the monitoring component is authenticated, which is the inversion the audit flagged. It also makes the approval's `approver` field unverifiable.

## Recommendation

**Option 1.** It is the minimum that makes an approval's approver field mean anything, and it is explicitly sized for a local single-user deployment rather than being a general identity system.

## What the operator must supply

Acceptance of the proposed shape, or a replacement. If replaced, state how the approver identity is established and how it is journalled.

## Default behaviour if unresolved

The approval-binding rung stops. Everything through the sealed immutable plan still proceeds — this is the correct cut point.

## Evidence required to close

A written authentication model recorded in the permission model.

## Cost of changing the decision later

High. Changing it later invalidates the authentication context bound into every existing approval.
