## The decision being requested

Which cryptographic hash algorithm does V1 use, and does the same choice govern the journal chain digest?

## Why it matters

This is the single highest-leverage decision in the project. It is baked into every contract, every stored fingerprint, every plan precondition, every approval binding, and every journal record. **It alone gates 67 of the 70 G3 rungs**, because the contracts rung cannot be frozen without it and almost everything depends on the contracts.

## Safe options

**1. SHA-256 for both content and chain**

One algorithm everywhere. The provisional recommendation already in the thresholds example.

*Tradeoff:* Slower than BLAKE3 on large media. Ubiquitous, hardware-accelerated on modern CPUs, and universally auditable.

**2. SHA-256 content, separate pinned chain digest**

Content hashing decided now; journal chain digest pinned independently so the chain is not hostage to a content-performance decision.

*Tradeoff:* Two algorithms to reason about, but decouples a performance question from an integrity question.

**3. BLAKE3 for content, SHA-256 for chain**

Faster content hashing on large corpora.

*Tradeoff:* Less ubiquitous tooling; an auditor may not have it to hand. Verification by a third party becomes harder.

## Recommendation

**Option 2.** Answer the chain-digest half now with SHA-256 and treat content hashing as a separate, revisitable performance question. Lane A observed that the journal chain, approval binding, and plan content hashing are all currently hostage to a decision framed as being about *content* hashing. Splitting them unblocks the contracts rung without pre-committing the media-hashing performance choice.

## What the operator must supply

The algorithm name and digest width for (a) file content and (b) the journal chain and content-addressed bindings. If they differ, say so explicitly.

## Default behaviour if unresolved

**Nothing proceeds.** FBL-002 cannot start, and 67 of 70 G3 rungs sit behind it. This is the hard stop.

## Evidence required to close

A written decision naming both algorithms, recorded in `open-decisions.md` and reflected in ADR-004.

## Cost of changing the decision later

**Very high.** Changing it later invalidates every stored fingerprint, every journal chain already written, and every approval binding. Treat as effectively irreversible once any data exists.
